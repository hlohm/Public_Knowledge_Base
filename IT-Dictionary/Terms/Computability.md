# Computability

> **Branch:** [[10 - Theory of Computation|Theory of Computation]]
> **Also known as:** Computability Theory

The study of what can be computed *at all*, resources aside. The boundary is real: well-defined problems exist — starting with the [[Halting Problem]] — that no algorithm can solve.

**Context.** The field's three pillars: the [[Turing Machine]] as the model, the [[Church-Turing Thesis]] claiming it captures 'computable,' and diagonalization proofs putting problems outside it. Practical residue everywhere: perfect malware detection, complete static analysis, and 'will this program ever crash?' are all undecidable in general — every real tool is an approximation, by mathematical necessity.
