# Message-ID

> **Branch:** [[05 - Internet & Web|Internet & Web]]

A header carrying a globally unique identifier for one message, of the form `<something-unique@domain.example>`, assigned once when the message is created and never changed afterwards.

**Context.** It is the only string that stays constant for a message end to end, which makes it do three jobs at once. Threading: replies carry `In-Reply-To:` and `References:` pointing at it, and that is how a client reconstructs a conversation. Deduplication: when the same message arrives twice by different routes — directly and via a [[Mailing List]] — the identifier is what lets a client show it once. Tracing: it is the handle you grep for across the logs of every hop, which in practice is how a delivery investigation actually gets done.

Two cautions. It is generated by the [[MUA]], or added by the [[MSA]] if the client omitted one, and it is not authenticated on its own — [[DKIM]] normally signs it, but absent that it is as forgeable as any header, so a matching Message-ID is not evidence of anything by itself. And naive generators leak: embedding the sending host's internal name, a timestamp or a predictable counter tells a recipient more about your infrastructure than you intended.
